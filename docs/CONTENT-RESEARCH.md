# Nest website: content research and editorial direction

Reviewed and implemented 2 October 2026. This is a local website revision, not a production deployment.

## Direction

The unifying story is **“Make room for what’s next.”** Nest connects the experiences customers see with the systems that fulfil the promise. OneSpace owns the operational story, MiraVoice the conversation, and NestCloud the hosting and continuity story. Web, mobile, marketing, and infrastructure have distinct jobs within that relationship.

OneSpace is a website within the Nest website: a landing page and five dedicated pages with local navigation, while retaining Nest’s global navigation, footer, visual language, contact details, and domain. It is described as Nest’s ERP service brand, not a newly invented proprietary ERP platform.

## Review scope and evidence

The review covered the live Nest homepage and OneSpace content, the workspace’s complete existing copy and route inventory, Axentor’s homepage, services, product catalogue, company and compliance content, and Data Value’s homepage, service, industry, module, FAQ and company page inventory. Linked resources were inspected where accessible. Search extraction returned cache errors on several Axentor product-detail pages and Data Value’s purchasing page; the accessible product catalogue and browser-rendered pages supplied the verified positioning. This is a review of publicly accessible business content, not an authenticated product audit, an exhaustive archive of every blog post, or verification of competitor delivery claims.

### Nest: breadth without enough decision support

[Current Nest website](https://www.nsd-eg.com/) and [OneSpace](https://www.nsd-eg.com/onespace).

The existing structure already establishes a broad technical partner and three specialist brands. Its most valuable differentiator is the ability to discuss applications, operations, communications, and hosting together. The new design already provides a strong warm palette, generous spacing, restrained typography, rounded artwork surfaces, and distinctive scroll behaviour; these were retained.

The content problem was specificity. The homepage led with architectural language, while service pages largely offered four short process steps. OneSpace was a long catalogue of ERPNext, Odoo, and Oracle features, with little explanation of who should choose what, implementation responsibilities, migration, adoption, or cost boundaries. Strong absolutes—unlimited capacity, absolute security, zero downtime, universal interoperability—created expectations without visible supporting terms.

Changes: replace superlatives with understandable business situations; explain implementation and ownership; preserve Odoo and Oracle as platform-selection conversations while giving ERPNext the requested depth; connect each service page to a useful next action. Existing client assets remain, but no new case study, testimonial, customer count, award, certification, or partnership claim was invented.

### Axentor: clarity and a coherent visual metaphor

Sources: [Homepage](https://axentor.co/), [What we do](https://axentor.co/what-we-do), [Products](https://axentor.co/products), [Egypt Compliance](https://axentor.co/compliance), [About](https://axentor.co/about).

Axentor organises its offer around implementation, improvement, and additional applications. Its product catalogue names platform and readiness, separating production offerings from early builds. The compliance page describes a recognisable invoice journey. Its tactile illustrations make operational concepts approachable.

The lesson for Nest is clear entry points and explicit scope. The new artwork uses original compositions, Nest’s cyan, and connected miniature environments. Competitor names, products, illustrations, results, and partner credentials are not presented as Nest assets. OneSpace’s broader connection to communications, hosting, and customer-facing products gives Nest its own narrative.

### Data Value: depth and buyer-oriented navigation

Sources: [Homepage](https://datavalue.solutions/), [Implementation](https://datavalue.solutions/services/implementation/), [Support](https://datavalue.solutions/services/support/), [Hosting](https://datavalue.solutions/services/hosting/), [E-invoicing](https://datavalue.solutions/services/e-invoicing/), [FAQs](https://datavalue.solutions/faqs/), [Company](https://datavalue.solutions/who-are-we/), [Success stories](https://datavalue.solutions/erpnext-success-story/).

Data Value exposes service, industry, module, and reference material as separate buyer paths. Implementation, hosting, integration, and ongoing support each receive specific attention. Industry pages describe recognisable workflows, while visible customer references provide proof. Its published commercial and certification claims belong to Data Value and were not transferred to Nest.

The opportunity is to offer equivalent navigational depth with a quieter reading experience and careful feature boundaries. Long feature lists, repeated claims, and visible pricing placeholders on the support page show why depth still needs editing. Industry-specific or tax-related statements on a competitor page were not treated as authoritative documentation.

Industry/module coverage inspected: [Manufacturing](https://datavalue.solutions/manufacturing-erpnext-management/), [Trading](https://datavalue.solutions/trading/), [Healthcare](https://datavalue.solutions/health/), [Construction](https://datavalue.solutions/real-estate-and-contracting/), [Education](https://datavalue.solutions/education/), [Tourism](https://datavalue.solutions/tourism/), [Accounting](https://datavalue.solutions/accounting-management/), [HR](https://datavalue.solutions/human-resources-management/), [CRM](https://datavalue.solutions/customer-relationship-management-crm/), and [Inventory](https://datavalue.solutions/inventory-management/).

## Site structure delivered

- `/`: business promise → familiar operational ambition → connected-business story → delivery approach → specialist brands → services → client logos → contact.
- `/onespace`: problem → order journey → three entry points → platform fit → deeper pages → FAQs → project enquiry.
- `/onespace/erpnext`: connected records, eight capability areas, configuration, distinct applications, licensing and ownership FAQs.
- `/onespace/industries`: eight expandable workflow areas; explicitly separates standard ERP, specialist applications and custom work.
- `/onespace/implementation`: blueprint, data preparation, user rehearsal, cutover, stabilisation, responsibilities and timing factors.
- `/onespace/integrations`: data ownership, system handovers, Egypt e-invoicing, e-receipts, custom applications and exception handling.
- `/onespace/support`: existing-system review, scope, incident expectations, upgrades and improvement.
- `/miravoice`: caller journey, team experience, reporting/recording boundaries and carrier questions.
- `/nestcloud`: workload, migration, ongoing operations, availability and data-location questions.
- `/web-development`: audience journey, application connections, launch and improvement.
- `/mobile-apps`: useful first release, real-world conditions, release readiness and maintenance.
- `/digital-marketing`: positioning, content/design/motion, meaningful measurement and commercial boundaries.
- `/infrastructure`: environment review, access boundaries, continuity and support responsibilities.
- `/about`: location, operating philosophy and the connected partnership.

Every substantive new page has a specific title and description, a canonical URL, a clear heading hierarchy, contextual actions and native expandable FAQs. New OneSpace routes are included in the sitemap. The contact form clearly describes its existing email-draft behaviour.

## Visual system

Four original raster illustrations were generated with the built-in image generation tool. The source images are preserved in `public/illustrations`: `onespace.png`, `voice.png`, `cloud.png`, and `digital.png`. They are used on the relevant service pages and the homepage. Next.js supplies responsive image delivery. No competitor artwork was downloaded or reused.

Art direction: warm ivory paper/ceramic miniature environments, navy details, translucent cyan connections, soft studio light, no embedded text. The images explain business relationships while fitting the existing design. Full prompts are in `IMAGE-PROMPTS.md`.

## Commercial facts still requiring owner validation before publication

The new content is a proposed description of delivery, not evidence of current operational capacity. Confirm that Nest offers the described workshops, training, integration maintenance, support arrangements, and recovery testing. Confirm actual supported platforms/editions, available Egypt connectors, hosting locations, language delivery, and scope boundaries. Pricing, contractual SLAs, client case studies and certification badges should only be added from approved evidence.

Existing client-logo permissions/relationships and the privacy/terms documents were not independently verified. Legal pages were preserved. No new customer outcomes or quantified business improvements were fabricated.

## Maintenance

`src/lib/content.ts` contains the shared brand and homepage copy. `src/lib/stories.ts` contains six service stories. `src/lib/onespace.ts` contains the microsite copy, modules and industry workflows. Keep future service claims and proof in those content sources. Review the ETA integration content when requirements or the selected connector change.
